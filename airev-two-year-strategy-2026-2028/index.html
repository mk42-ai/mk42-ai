02Thesis

OnDemand is the ARM
of the agentic era.

Arm never built a phone. It licensed the architecture into every chip and collected a royalty on each one shipped. OnDemand is licensed into the silicon the world already buys — and collects a royalty on every activated device. Models rotate underneath; the harness stays.

What must be true for the analogy to hold — the two-year job

  • Neutral across silicon. Qualcomm NPU, Intel CPU/NPU, Tenstorrent Tensix, NVIDIA GPU (rented) — one harness, certified images for each. ●
  • Activation curve, not signatures. The TLA carries no minimum commitment; 2027 must show ≥279K devices activating — the KPI the next round is priced on. ◐
  • Renewal annuity. Channel renewals ~35% of year-1 fees; IFRS 15 splits the $100 into licence (point-in-time) and 36-month support (deferred), so billings lead revenue. IM
  • Below-the-model position. As frontier models commoditise, the harness holds the customer; sovereignty (air-gapped, in-country) is the wedge peers cannot retrofit. IM
Arm figures: Arm Holdings 10-K for the fiscal year ended 31 March 2026 and arm.com (99% smartphone share; >350B chips), royalty-rate colour per Arm investor materials. AIREV figures: Investment Memorandum §7–§9. The analogy is a positioning device; Arm's royalties are a percentage of ASP on billions of units, OnDemand's are a fixed fee on hundreds of thousands — the plan's first job is the activation curve.

Use this card to pre-empt the obvious objection: we are not claiming Arm's scale, we are claiming Arm's mechanism — licence in, royalty out, neutral to the vendor above and below.
